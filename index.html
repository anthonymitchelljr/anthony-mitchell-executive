Executive Perspective

My first five months as a CIO

A CIO mandate requires more than a traditional 90-day plan. My approach balances listening, enterprise risk reduction, operational stabilization, strategic alignment, and measurable execution across the first five months.

Month 1

Listen, assess, and establish trust

Meet with the CEO, executive team, board stakeholders, business leaders, and IT staff. Assess enterprise risk, service performance, cybersecurity posture, talent, vendor dependencies, architecture, and major business priorities.

Month 2

Stabilize operations and reduce immediate risk

Address critical reliability, security, compliance, continuity, and service-delivery gaps. Clarify ownership, strengthen incident readiness, validate recovery capabilities, and improve visibility into technology performance.

Month 3

Align technology with enterprise strategy

Translate business priorities into an executive technology agenda. Establish governance, define investment principles, identify quick wins, and align the technology portfolio with growth, customer experience, workforce productivity, and risk tolerance. Partner with the CFO to review technology spend, contract obligations, cloud consumption, capital priorities, and forecast assumptions.

Month 4

Publish the roadmap and mobilize execution

Present a sequenced multi-year roadmap with clear outcomes, dependencies, investment requirements, and accountability. Launch priority modernization, cybersecurity, data, cloud, and operating-model initiatives.

Month 5

Measure progress and build transformation momentum

Establish executive dashboards, confirm value realization, strengthen team capability, refine sourcing and vendor strategy, and create a repeatable operating cadence that sustains accountability and long-term transformation. Track budget performance, savings realization, vendor commitments, cloud costs, and return on major technology investments with the CFO.

IT Thought Leadership

Perspectives on compliance, cybersecurity, and executive technology leadership

Selected insights from Anthony L. Mitchell Jr. on the disciplines required to build secure, resilient, and trusted technology organizations.

Compliance & Governance

Compliance Should Strengthen the Business—Not Slow It Down

Effective compliance programs do more than prepare an organization for an audit. They clarify accountability, improve decision-making, reduce operational risk, and create repeatable practices that support sustainable growth.

“Compliance is strongest when it becomes part of how the business operates—not a last-minute exercise before an audit.”

Cybersecurity

Cybersecurity Is a Leadership Responsibility

Security cannot be delegated entirely to the IT department. Executives must understand the organization’s risk posture, define acceptable risk, fund the right controls, and create a culture where protection and accountability are shared.

“Cybersecurity becomes effective when leadership treats it as a business priority, not merely a technical function.”

Executive Leadership

Technology Leaders Must Build Trust Before They Build Transformation

Transformation succeeds when leaders create clarity, communicate honestly, and earn confidence across the organization. Before people support a new roadmap, they must trust the leader responsible for delivering it.

“Transformation may begin with technology, but it succeeds through trust.”

Executive Leadership

The Best IT Leaders Translate Complexity Into Confidence

Executive teams do not need more technical vocabulary. They need clear choices, understandable risk, practical recommendations, and confidence that technology decisions support the mission of the organization.

“Executive presence is not about knowing every answer. It is about creating clarity when the path forward is complex.”
